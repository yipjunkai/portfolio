## Overall Impression

The evidence layer is genuinely good and the framing layer is generic — and the site is arranged so a real visitor mostly meets the framing layer. The proof and the conversion action never appear on the same screen, and the surface most people land on has neither. Meanwhile a documented accessibility defect that DESIGN.md itself flagged as "recorded here so it is fixed rather than propagated" has been propagated, and is worse than documented: five of six taxonomy badges fail contrast, not three.

Biggest opportunity: make the home page carry the evidence. Everything else is downstream of a hero that spends a full viewport establishing an identity the visitor already has.

## What's Working

1. The dossier entry header is a real invention. ExperienceCard.tsx:37-57. The register switch is load-bearing — mono answers where/who/when, sans answers what is claimed. Scales unchanged across jobs, research, education without a container. The linked employer in blue rather than the brand gradient is correct: an outbound link is not a brand moment.

2. The code-block system earns its status as the signature surface. Dual-theme Shiki with per-token CSS vars and !important documented as needed to beat Tailwind prose's --tw-prose-pre-code; bypassInlineCode so inline code stays a chip; defaultLang scoped to blocks only. All 50 blocks on the VM post swap correctly. The comments explaining why each decision exists are the positioning claim made visible.

3. The email dialog handles the highest-stakes moment correctly. Two paths — mailto: for readers with a client, copy for those without. The divider names the choice. Copy confirmation keyed on a counter so pressing twice re-fires. aria-label, title, motion-reduce:hidden present. The one place the site anticipates a reader off the happy path — and it exists on exactly one page.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The hero CTA pair can vanish on hydration, taking both conversion paths with it

What. PDFDialogContent is dynamic(() => import(...), { ssr: false }) (ResumePDFDialog.tsx:9-11) rendered as a sibling of DialogTrigger inside Radix's Dialog. Server emits [DialogTrigger, nothing]; client emits [<Suspense>, ...]. Child order diverges, React bails to client rendering, and the rebuild loses BOTH triggers — EmailMeDialog's button is collateral damage because it shares the flex container. Assessment A captured the empty container: heroLastChildHTML: "<div class=\"flex flex-col gap-4 ... lg:flex-row\"></div>", namedButtons: [].

Honest caveat — the assessments disagree. A reproduced it on 1 of 5 loads with the React diff naming <LoadableComponent> / <Suspense fallback={null}> vs <button data-slot="dialog-trigger">. B, reading 2277 console messages with onlyErrors and hydration filters, found ZERO hydration errors. An earlier session captured a full BAILOUT_TO_CLIENT_SIDE_RENDERING stack trace. Read together: real and intermittent, a race between the dynamic chunk resolving and hydration starting — so it fires more often on slow connections, exactly the mobile lookup PRODUCT.md describes. Frequency is unknown, not settled.

Why it matters. PRODUCT.md defines success as an inbound message and names the resume dialog and email as the two paths that matter. When this fires, both are gone from the only page carrying them, with no error and no fallback.

Fix. Gate mounting on open state so the component is never in the server tree: const [open, setOpen] = useState(false) and {open && <PDFDialogContent ... />}. Also fixes the missing loading state, since the dialog surface can own a skeleton instead of fallback={null}.
Suggested command: /impeccable harden

### [P1] The accessibility floor is below WCAG AA in eight measured places — including the defect DESIGN.md already documented

What. Both assessments measured contrast independently and agree within rounding. White text at 14px on the taxonomy fills: database 1.91:1 FAIL, frontend 2.22:1 FAIL, service 2.89:1 FAIL, language 3.76:1 FAIL, backend 4.12:1 FAIL, other 4.84:1 pass.

DESIGN.md names three of these and says "This is a known defect in the incumbent implementation, recorded here so it is fixed rather than propagated." It has been propagated — badge.tsx:14-19 still ships bg-yellow-500 text-white — and it is worse than documented: Language Blue and Service Orange are undocumented failures.

Five more, all found by the deterministic pass:

- Secondary button label, default size: gradient-clipped coral on white card = 3.06:1 at 14px, fails. Same button.tsx:19 the detector flagged as an AI-tell false positive — sanctioned construction, real defect. Dark mode was already fixed for exactly this reason; light mode never was. Affects EmailMeDialog.tsx:28, pdf-dialog-content.tsx:69, error.tsx:20.
- Brand gradient badge with white text: coral end 3.06:1 at 14px — blog "Guides" chip and both research tags.
- Blog TOC rail, BlogTocRail.tsx:156: inactive text-neutral-500 on canvas = 3.83:1 light (dark passes 7.66:1).
- Shiki dark-theme comments: 3.05:1 — 59 of 600 sampled tokens fail on the VM post in dark; 0 of 600 fail in light.
- Theme toggle focus ring, ThemeChanger.tsx:47: ring-white/80 + outline-none composites to 1.19:1 against the light sidebar. WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 needs 3:1, and outline-none removes the UA fallback.

Structural failures alongside: 96 of 165 interactive elements on a blog post are under 44x44 (theme toggle 28x28, language toggle 40x40, nav items 167x40, TOC links ~159x20). The closed mobile drawer has opacity:0 but no aria-hidden and no inert, so 10 off-screen links accept focus (MobileTopNav.tsx:51-58). /experience skips h1 → h3.

Why it matters. A founder reading on a laptop in a bright room cannot read the stack, and badges are the fastest signal a technical reader scans. More damaging: a site whose claim is engineering discipline shipping a defect its own design system flagged for repair.

Fix. Darken the five fills to -600/-700, or invert to tinted background + dark text (better suits the near-monochrome system). Replace the light-mode gradient text fill with solid ink, mirroring dark. Give the gradient badge dark text. Fix the TOC to neutral-600. Swap ring-white/80 for ring-ring/50 per DESIGN.md's stated 3px standard. Add inert to the closed drawer. Insert the missing h2.
Suggested command: /impeccable audit, then /impeccable colorize

### [P2] The decorative layer is fighting the evidence layer, and it has spent the gradient budget on chrome

What. Four connected deviations from Named Rules, with detector and design review agreeing:

1. TechPattern watermarks collide with body text. absolute ... md:top-1/2 md:-translate-y-1/2 at opacity-20 (TechPattern.tsx:312) centres a 280x280 SVG behind each entry. Observed: Kipo circuit through bullets 1-3 and the Languages/Frontend/Backend rows; Gigworks wireframe through "...a ~6-month delivery gap..."; DSBJ node graph through the date. Stroked in var(--color-grad-1)/--color-grad-2 — the brand gradient as a large decorative field, which the One Gradient Rule forbids. Overlay flagged shape-assembled-illustration x2.
2. An animated glow at rest on the theme toggle (ThemeChanger.tsx:38), disabled only at lg. The Flat-At-Rest Rule bans glow at rest outright. Breakpoints diverge: the primary CTA's glow dies at md (768px), the toggle's at lg (1024px) — so between 768px and 1023px the loudest pulsing object on the page is the theme switcher while the conversion CTA is flat. Verified in button.tsx:13.
3. The gradient appears in seven places against a documented budget of four — adding theme-toggle track, its halo, the language button, and the hamburger's lower bar. Overlay's ai-color-palette fired 5-7 times per page.
4. ExtensionPopupPreview.tsx imports a whole foreign visual language — all 12 detector findings live here, plus shadow-2xl ring-1 at rest, rounded-xl (a third radius, against the 8/10 Rule), and orange-500/amber-500 fills outside the palette. Overlay added nested-cards x3 and gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow x2 on the same page.

Why it matters. Scarcity is what makes the gradient mean "act here." Right now the two most chromatically loud objects on any page are the theme and language switchers — the least consequential controls on a confirmation surface — while the watermarks degrade legibility of the exact bullets the reader came to verify.

Fix. Move TechPattern outside the 900px measure, or drop to ~6% opacity in hairline neutral. Strip the halo and gradient fill from ThemeChanger and LanguageChanger; make them ghost controls. Replace shadow-2xl ring-1 with a 1px hairline at 10px radius. Either register the popup mock's type scale as a documented exception or bring it onto the ramp.
Suggested command: /impeccable quieter, then /impeccable polish

## Questions to Consider

1. What if the home page were the evidence page? Four routes serve one visitor with one question. If / carried five walkable proof lines and the CTA pair, would /experience and /projects become the depth layer Principle 4 says should be "available without being mandatory"?
2. Should "View resume" be the loud button at all? Success is an inbound message. A PDF download is a deferral — it lets the reader leave with a file and decide later. Right now the halo, the gradient fill, and the first tab stop all point at the deferral.
3. What would the site look like if it were built to be entered at any URL? Every structural weakness above assumes a reader who enters at / and traverses — which PRODUCT.md says is not how they arrive.
4. Is the six-colour taxonomy earning its cost? Five of six fail contrast, it is the loudest thing on two pages, DESIGN.md needs a whole Named Rule fencing it off, and its job is already done by the text label beside every row.
5. The unwritten claim. The VM post has a section titled "Threat model." PRODUCT.md states explicitly that no standalone threat model exists and copy must not imply one. Is that section the strongest version of the claim, or the site's copy drifting past its own line?
